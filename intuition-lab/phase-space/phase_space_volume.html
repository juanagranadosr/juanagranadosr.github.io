The one equation behind all of it

Transport a density ρ with the flow: ∂ₜρ + ∇·(ρ f) = 0. Along a trajectory this reads dρ/dt = −(∇·f) ρ, and the Jacobian determinant of the flow map obeys d ln det Dφₜ / dt = tr J = ∇·f. The dashed curve integrates exactly this, per sample point; the solid curve measures the boundary geometrically. They agree because they are the same theorem.

For Hamiltonian flow, J = ε · Hess H, and the trace of an antisymmetric matrix times a symmetric one vanishes: Liouville. For gradient flow, J = −Hess V is symmetric, so there is no rotation anywhere. The hover panel splits J into its symmetric part (stretching, whose trace is ∇·f) and its antisymmetric part (rotation Ω).
